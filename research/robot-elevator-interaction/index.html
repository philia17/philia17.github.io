Robot–Elevator Interaction: From Physical Panel Recognition to Activation

Siheng Ye · Zejing Tong · Suiyi Zhang · David Chieng

Abstract

Existing robot-accessible elevator interfaces require manufacturer-specific retrofits which are costly and difficult to scale. Direct physical interaction with existing elevator panels offers a promising infrastructure-independent alternative, but requires robots to reliably perceive, interpret, and actuate controls despite variations in panel layouts, lighting conditions, reflections, and labeling conventions. We present an edge-based prototype system with an end-to-end perception-to-action pipeline that integrates task-specific button detection, specialized target recognition, vision-language semantic understanding, confidence-aware rejection, semantic-state maintenance, asynchronous processing, and physical button actuation. We evaluate perception performance, static-image target selection, runtime behaviour, and physical actuation through two qualitative button-activation demonstrations. Across 475 target-selection requests derived from 112 static RGB images spanning six buildings, the system selected the intended button in 404 cases, achieving an accuracy of 85.1%. On a separate benchmark of 379 human-annotated Open/Close/Other button crops, the HOG+SVM classifier achieved a macro-F1 score of 0.9963. During a 207-s runtime evaluation, the system filtered 99.8% of redundant candidate-level semantic requests, with no observed label switching, flicker, crashes, or camera interruptions. In both operator-initiated physical button-activation demonstrations, the robotic arm activated the selected button, withdrew, and returned to its standby state. These results provide practical insights and design considerations for the development of robust, edge-based, offline, and real-time robot–elevator interaction systems.
