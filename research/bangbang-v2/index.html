Project overview

Bangbang is a collaborative tele-operated avatar-robot platform. Facial-expression and bilingual speech cues support an operator who can inspect the interaction and choose how to respond.

The work includes a perception study (HRFEST Paper 230) and a related operator-assistance study (IHCI Paper 99). The submitted FURP 2026 poster brings these strands together.

My contribution

My work focused on the facial-perception pipeline and its Raspberry Pi 5 integration: a shared MediaPipe facial-landmark front end, a learned CNN/ONNX route and an interpretable action-unit route.

The wider team contributed speech, operator-interface and teleoperation components. The perception outputs describe visible movement; they are not validated measurements of internal emotion or a clinical condition.

Prototype and scope

The public release contains facial-landmark and action-unit processing, emotion-model definitions, training/export scripts and Raspberry Pi vision examples. Datasets and trained model files are not bundled.

An earlier stationary speech-and-vision branch uses Vosk with an English example vocabulary. It is retained separately from the later vision work and does not reproduce the complete bilingual speech or teleoperation system described in the team research.

The related Paper 99 presentation shows local and cloud AI assistance, bilingual input and a browser interface for reviewing suggested replies. Chinese speech recognition uses a cloud service. Its timing figures are development observations with varying prompts and settings; they do not form a controlled model ranking.

Functional checks and demonstrations show particular prototype functions. They do not establish end-to-end reliability, accurate interpretation of emotion, reduced operator workload or user benefit. Those questions need further evaluation.
